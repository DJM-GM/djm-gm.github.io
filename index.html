Bunk of Dad and Mum™

FastSaver Home Buying Calculator — Australia

This calculator is about the Bigger Picture: with FastSaving and some patience, owning a home outright — or saving a large deposit in just a few months — may be well within reach.

FastSaving means directing a large share of after-tax income — typically 80% — straight into savings by keeping everyday expenses small. The key is to save faster than home prices rise. One proven way to do this is the “Bunk of Dad and Mum”: parents provide rent-free accommodation to two residents (typically their adult children) while the residents save diligently, share household expenses, and cover their own costs. This arrangement is far less risky for the parents than the “Bank of Mum and Dad”, where parents gift or lend money toward a purchase — the Bunk costs the parents very little and leaves their own finances intact.

The Bunk works best when there is a Window of Opportunity: the residents are earning well, the family home can still comfortably accommodate everyone, and there is time before the parents downsize or need the space back. Other routes to the same outcome include employer-provided accommodation or any other low-cost living arrangement.

Buying without a mortgage eliminates negative equity risk entirely. It also buys time — time to understand exactly what kind of home suits your needs, and to wait for a genuine bargain rather than rushing into a purchase. If saving a full purchase price takes longer than you’d like, a large deposit is the next best thing: it reduces borrowing, lowers repayments, and protects against falling prices.

Default example: Two FastSavers on average wages, saving 80% of after-tax income, could accumulate enough to buy a mortgage-free home worth around $600,000 in 48 months — or save a 20% deposit in as little as 8 months.

Set Term to your target month and adjust the inputs to see projected net worth, cash, and home value at end of Term. If you already own a home, enter its value and any outstanding mortgage — the calculator treats the equity as though the home were sold at month 0 and the proceeds added to savings. Browse real estate sites regularly to stay current on home and rent prices in your area. Vary Term, Income Saved Portion and other parameters until the results, such as living expenses, net worth, estimated home value, months to having adequate deposit are a realistic and satisfactory compromise. The parameter values are saved in the browser local storage with each Recalculate.

Purchase costs vary and some are hard to predict. When you are close to buying, use separate calculators for Stamp Duty, First Home Owner Grant, and Lenders Mortgage Insurance, then enter the combined figure in Buyers Up Front Costs. Add estimates for building inspections and moving costs as needed. There is no need to be precise — a reasonable round number is fine. It will not change the Big Picture much. FastSaving moves the graph far more than any reasonable cost estimate.

“Isn’t saving 80% the same as mortgage stress?” For most people, a mortgage consuming 80% of income is stressful. FastSavers are different: they have chosen this path, they are building wealth rather than servicing debt, and they learn to live well on less by spending only on what they need and finding the best available value.

Other Methods

For different calculations with nearly the same result, the Future Value of savings with the monthly bank deposit increasing and earning monthly interest can be calculated using the Growing Annuity formula and the Continuous Compounding formula. Both are close approximations to the iterative transaction calculation of the bank balance. Not so good at calculating the bank — mortgage — offset balances.
